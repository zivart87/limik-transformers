## 8. Specifying a Transformer

**Section H2:** 
Specifying a Utility Power Transformer

**Intro Paragraph:** 
Specifying a grid-scale transformer is a strict matching exercise: balancing nameplate capacity against short-circuit duties, legacy substation layouts, and long-term modernization plans. Below is the core criteria our engineering team reviews for utility projects.

**Card 1:**
*H3:* MVA / kVA Rating
*P:* Present load, forecasted regional electrification growth, and emergency overload capabilities. Under-sizing risks peak-season failures; over-sizing wastes capital and increases lifetime no-load losses.

**Card 2:**
*H3:* Voltage Class & BIL
*P:* Primary matched to transmission lines (up to 525 kV) and secondary to sub-transmission or distribution networks (13.8 kV, 34.5 kV, 69 kV). Basic Impulse Level (BIL) is strictly engineered for your specific lightning and switching surge environment.

**Card 3:**
*H3:* Cooling Classification
*P:* ONAN, ONAF, ODAF, or OFAF. We match the cooling stage design to your site's ambient temperature extremes, noise ordinance restrictions, and peak load capacity requirements.

**Card 4:**
*H3:* Voltage Regulation & LTCs
*P:* Grid stability requires precise voltage control under fluctuating loads. We engineer robust On-Load Tap Changers (LTC) and De-Energized Tap Changers (DETC) customized to your network's specific regulation profile.

**Card 5:**
*H3:* Footprint & Bushing Configuration
*P:* For retrofit projects, physical dimensions are non-negotiable. We match legacy pad sizes, phase spacing, and bushing layouts (cover or side-mounted) so the new unit drops in without structural substation rebuilds.
