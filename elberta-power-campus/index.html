How the site is used

  1. Power island on 61:094:0027. The turbines sit between the 345 kV line and the Williams pipeline, so the gas lateral and the grid tie are both short.
  2. Gas engines beside them. On the east edge of 61:095:0010, sharing the gas header and the collector substation.
  3. Data center on 61:095:0010. 60 blocks of four modular units, east of the Kern River easement.
  4. Access from US-6. Tunnel Rd on the east; the 35:104:0004 strip gives a second way in from the west.
  5. Reserve. West of Kern River on 0010, plus parcels 0003, 0004 and 0028, stay free for later phases, water and storage.

To verify

  • Frequency. The SGT5-4000F is a 50 Hz machine and the US grid is 60 Hz. Its 60 Hz sibling is the SGT6-5000F, about 260 MW. Confirm the model with Siemens Energy or the seller.
  • Altitude. At about 4,900 ft a gas turbine gives roughly 15% less than its sea-level rating; engines lose less.
  • Gas volume. 600 MW needs roughly 120 to 130 million cubic feet a day. Confirm taps and capacity with Kern River and MountainWest.
  • Pipeline and line positions. Drawn from the broker flyer. The screening map puts the gas line further east on 61:095:0010, so a survey and an 811 locate are needed.
  • Grid. The screening tool shows limited headroom on the 345 kV line, which is why the campus is sized to generate its own power.
  • Air permit for this much gas generation, and water for the turbines if run as a combined cycle.
