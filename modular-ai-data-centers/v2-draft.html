Grid & power transformer

Transformer lead times hold back many AI projects. LIMIK builds its own medium-voltage transformers, so power supply is planned with the module, not ordered separately.

Distribution & backup power

Switchgear, UPS and batteries are part of the module and sized to its IT load. Redundant UPS modules keep compute running through a single failure; generators connect as an option.

Liquid cooling

Direct-to-chip liquid cooling removes heat at the chip, as today's dense GPU racks require. Coolant units and dry coolers include a spare and are sized to your climate.

Compute & controls

LIMIK sources and installs servers, GPUs, networking and storage with IT partners. One monitoring system covers the whole module, from grid to coolant.

AI is the main focus. The same modules also suit high-performance computing, simulation, rendering, business applications and storage. The base design is liquid-cooled; fully air-cooled hardware needs a separate capacity review.

Core 20: two racks, 100 kW IT, on existing 480 V site service. Core 40: six racks, 600 kW IT, with a planned 1.25 MVA LIMIK medium-voltage transformer. Core Campus: several Core 40 modules, with transformers sized to the site. Share your target capacity and available power, and we suggest a starting configuration.

It can be assessed for your site: grid supply, on-site generation or a combination. We review source capacity, protection, transfer equipment and permits.

Core 40 supports compute racks up to 145 kW with direct-to-chip liquid cooling: four compute positions plus two 10 kW network or storage positions within a 600 kW IT budget. Core 20 has two 50 kW positions. Air cooling handles the remaining components, and heat rejection is sized to your climate. We check your specific GPU racks before the layout is fixed.

UPS and cooling components are N+1: if one unit fails, the IT load keeps running, with about 5 minutes of battery for critical loads. Two independent power paths, longer autonomy and generators are available as separate design options.

Delivery timing is project-specific. It is set after scope, engineering requirements, supplier lead times and site readiness are confirmed; the concept has no fixed public lead time.

The planned turnkey scope covers selected servers, GPUs, networking and storage, the enclosure and rack positions, power distribution, UPS and batteries, cooling and external heat rejection, monitoring, access control, leak and fire protection; Core 40 also includes a planned LIMIK transformer. Options include generators, longer battery autonomy, extra redundancy and heat recovery.
