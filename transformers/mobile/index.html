The LIMIK Mobile Platform

A LIMIK mobile substation is a complete, transportable substation on a single trailer: power transformer, high- and low-voltage switchgear, protection relays, and controls — integrated, tested, and ready to energize on arrival. Where a fixed substation takes months to build, a mobile unit restores power in days.

Every unit is engineered to your system — voltage class, MVA rating, protection scheme, and trailer configuration — and built to move. Size and weight are optimized against road restrictions without compromising the robustness the grid demands. Each mobile is factory-tested to IEEE C57.12.90 before it ships.

Need the transformer alone? LIMIK also supplies mobile power transformers — the transformer unit on its own, for utilities that already have switchgear and controls in place. A mobile substation is a turnkey system; a mobile transformer is a single component. We build both, to your specification.

LIMIK builds and sells mobile substations and transformers to own — we don't operate a rental fleet. The mobile substation market genuinely splits into two models: rental providers who supply short-term equipment for a single outage or planned project, and manufacturers who build a unit that becomes part of your permanent spare fleet. If you need equipment for a one-time, short-duration deployment, a rental fleet may be the faster fit. If you're building standing emergency-response capacity you'll use repeatedly, owning a unit engineered to your system typically pays for itself over multiple deployments.

Both approaches are common, and the right one depends on your fleet. Utilities with several substations at similar voltage and MVA levels often specify one flexible spare — sometimes with a wider tap range — sized to serve as backup across multiple sites. Utilities with a single critical, larger, or non-standard substation more often specify a spare matched closely to that one unit's voltage ratio and impedance. Both the HV/LV voltage combination and the MVA rating need to line up with what you're backing up; our engineering team can help model which strategy fits your substation fleet.

Mobile substations are engineered for mobile, temporary-duty service — trailer-mounted construction and duty cycle are built around being deployed, energized, and eventually relocated, not around the multi-decade fixed installation a permanent substation is designed for. That said, there's no universal fixed time limit on how long one can stay in place; utilities commonly run a mobile unit as a bridge for months while a permanent replacement is engineered and built, and the actual duration is governed by your own interconnection agreement and system planning, not a hard rule from the manufacturer.

Weight and space are constrained on a trailer-mounted unit in a way they aren't on a fixed substation transformer, and an on-load tap changer adds both. Many mobile deployments — emergency restoration, temporary construction power — don't require continuous under-load voltage regulation, so a simpler, lighter configuration is often the better fit for the application. Where your project's protection scheme or grid interconnection agreement calls for on-load regulation, OLTC is available as a specified option.

If you already have switchgear and controls that meet your protection requirements, a standalone mobile transformer is engineered to interface with what you have. If you're not yet sure whether your existing equipment is compatible, or you may need a fully integrated unit down the line, it's worth discussing both scenarios with our engineering team before you finalize a specification — reworking the interface later costs more than planning for it up front.
